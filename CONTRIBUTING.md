# Contributing

Read [POLICY.md](POLICY.md) first. It is short, and it is the part of this
project that carries the weight.

## Adding someone

1. Check they are not already in `people/`.
2. Find the sources **before** writing the entry. If you cannot find two
   independent primary sources, stop — the entry cannot be added yet, and that
   is the correct outcome rather than a problem to work around.
3. Copy `people/_example.yml` to `people/<id>.yml`.
4. Run `bun run validate`.
5. Open a pull request. Fill in the checklist honestly.

### On writing the summary

Write it as though the person's family will read it, because they might.

State what they built and what it meant. Two or three sentences is usually
enough — this is not a résumé and not an obituary, it is a marker that says
*this person was here and this is what they left*.

Do not write about how they died. Do not write about their health, their
personal life, or anything they did not make public themselves. If the family
has spoken publicly about the circumstances and it is part of how the person is
remembered, a single factual clause is the most that belongs here.

Avoid the register of a press release. No "visionary", no "revolutionised", no
"legendary". Plain description carries more.

## On sources

The bar is: **would a stranger, following your links, independently conclude
this person has died?**

- Two links from the same site are one source.
- A news article that cites a family blog post is not independent of that post.
- Wikipedia and Wikidata are places to *find* citations, not citations
  themselves. Follow them to the underlying source and cite that.
- Archive every source you can (`archived:` field). Personal sites go offline,
  and often it is the death itself that takes them offline.

## What we will reject

- Entries built on inactivity, a quiet account, or an unanswered email.
- Entries sourced only to wikis, aggregators, or other memorial lists.
- Entries about someone whose death is rumoured but not confirmed.
- Anything generated by an AI tool without a human checking every source. Using
  a tool to *draft* is fine; submitting text nobody verified is not.

If you are unsure whether your evidence is enough, open an issue and ask before
writing the entry. Asking is never unwelcome.

## Corrections

Open a pull request or an issue. Corrections from family or colleagues are
taken at their word and do not need sourcing — if someone who knew the person
says a detail is wrong, it is wrong.

## Translations

Entries are written in English. Translations live alongside the entry and are
listed in the `languages` field. A translation must not add claims the original
does not make.
